Section 01

Introduction to CRM Systems

1.1 What is Customer Relationship Management (CRM)?

Customer Relationship Management (CRM) is the process of managing a company's interactions and relationships with its customers and other stakeholders.

A Customer Relationship Management system is software that helps an organisation collect, store, organise and analyse customer information so that it can provide better services, improve customer relationships and increase sales.

CRM systems commonly use databases together with other software tools to manage customer interactions.

📌 Key definition — CRM system

A software system that manages customer information and interactions to improve customer satisfaction, encourage customer loyalty and increase business profitability.

For example, a supermarket may use a CRM system to record:

  • Customers' names and contact details.
  • Products customers frequently purchase.
  • Dates and values of previous purchases.
  • Customer loyalty points and rewards.
  • Customer enquiries, complaints and feedback.
  • Customers' preferences and buying habits.

The supermarket can analyse this information to understand its customers and provide personalised services.

1.2 Main purposes of CRM systems

Purpose Explanation
Improve customer relationships Understand customers' needs and preferences.
Improve customer satisfaction Provide appropriate and personalised services.
Increase sales Recommend suitable products and encourage additional purchases.
Encourage customer loyalty Reward customers and motivate them to make repeat purchases.
Improve marketing Send targeted advertisements and promotional messages.
Support business decisions Analyse customer information and buying patterns.

1.3 How does a CRM system work?

A CRM system typically collects information from different parts of an organisation, stores and analyses that information, and uses the results to support business activities.

Section 02

Synchronising Marketing Events

2.1 What is marketing?

Marketing refers to the activities a business carries out to promote its products and services and encourage customers to purchase them.

Businesses often communicate with customers through several platforms, such as social media, email, SMS and websites. A business may need to advertise the same promotion across all these platforms.

2.2 What does synchronising marketing events mean?

📌 Key definition — Marketing synchronisation

Coordinating marketing activities so that advertising and promotional messages are consistent and delivered at appropriate times across different communication platforms.

A CRM system allows businesses to plan, schedule and automate related marketing activities from one central system. For example, a clothing store might plan a weekend promotion offering a 20% discount.

Diagram · One promotion, multiple channels
📅 CRM Marketing SchedulePlan, coordinate and automate a promotion
↓
✉️EmailDiscount announcement
💬SMSPromotion reminder
📱Social mediaScheduled posts

All marketing activities are coordinated through the same CRM system.

2.3 How does a CRM system help?

🗓️

Centralised planning

Marketing activities can be organised within a single system instead of being managed separately.

⏰

Automated scheduling

Promotional messages can be sent at predetermined times without manual sending.

✅

Consistent messages

Customers receive the same promotional information across different platforms.

🔗

Platform integration

CRM tools connect to other systems, such as social media services, to distribute messages.

In addition, better coordination helps marketing teams and other departments work together effectively.

💡 Example — Supermarket weekend promotion

A supermarket launches a special discount on Friday. Its CRM system schedules an email on Thursday, a social media advertisement on Friday morning, and an SMS reminder on Saturday. All messages promote the same offer and encourage shoppers to visit during the promotion.

Section 03

Loyalty Schemes

3.1 What is a loyalty scheme?

📌 Key definition — Loyalty scheme

A marketing strategy that rewards customers for purchasing products or services from a business, encouraging them to return rather than shop with competitors.

Businesses may offer loyalty cards, reward points, vouchers and special discounts. The main aim is to encourage customers to continue purchasing from the same business.

3.2 How do loyalty schemes work?

A loyalty scheme often awards points according to the value of purchases. Customers can accumulate points and exchange them for rewards later.

Example · Loyalty points
🛍️$50Customer purchase
⭐50 pointsPoints earned
🎁RewardRedeem points later

Illustrative scheme: $1 spent earns 1 point. Actual rewards and rules vary by business.

This gives customers a reason to return to the same store, instead of buying from competitors.

3.3 How are CRM systems used in loyalty schemes?

CRM systems are often linked to other business systems:

🧾

EPOS systems

Electronic Point of Sale systems record the products purchased and the value of each transaction.

📦

Inventory databases

Store information about products and the stock available to the organisation.

👤

Customer databases

Store customer profiles, purchase histories, preferences and loyalty points.

📊

Data analysis

Identifies buying patterns to provide suitable incentives to different customers.

3.4 Personalised loyalty rewards

Basic loyalty schemes provide general rewards to everyone. More effective loyalty schemes use CRM data to offer personalised incentives based on customers' buying habits.

💡 Example — Breakfast cereal voucher
  1. A customer regularly buys a particular brand of breakfast cereal.
  2. The EPOS system records the purchase against the customer's loyalty account.
  3. The CRM system analyses the purchase history.
  4. The system identifies the customer's preferred cereal brand.
  5. The store sends a personalised discount voucher for that brand.
  6. The customer is encouraged to return and buy the cereal again.

3.5 Benefits of loyalty schemes

Benefit Explanation
Encourages repeat purchases Customers return to earn or redeem rewards.
Improves customer loyalty Customers feel rewarded for continuing to shop.
Provides valuable data Businesses learn about customer buying habits.
Supports targeted offers Customers receive promotions relevant to their interests.
Increases sales opportunities Discounts and rewards may encourage additional purchases.

Section 04

Analysing Buying Trends

4.1 What are buying trends?

📌 Key definition — Buying trends

Patterns in customers' purchasing behaviour that can be identified by analysing sales data over time.

Buying trends may show:

  • Which products customers purchase frequently.
  • Which products are becoming more or less popular.
  • When customers are most likely to purchase particular products.
  • Which customer groups prefer particular products.
  • How demand changes at different times of the year.

4.2 How does CRM analyse buying trends?

CRM systems analyse information generated by sales transactions. By looking for patterns in this data, businesses can make informed decisions about stock levels, advertising and product promotions.

For example, a supermarket may discover that demand for cold drinks increases during hot weather. This information can help with stock purchasing and promotional planning.

4.3 How do buying trends support business decisions?

📦

1. Stock purchasing decisions

Determine which products to buy and how much stock may be needed. This can reduce overstocking and shortages.

📅

2. Timing of advertisements

Identify when customers are most likely to be interested in particular products and schedule promotions accordingly.

🎯

3. Targeting appropriate customers

Direct advertisements towards customers who are more likely to want the advertised products.

💼

4. Strategic decisions

Use patterns in customer purchasing data to support longer-term business planning.

4.4 Practical example — Clothing store

💡 Example — Demand for jackets

A clothing store examines previous sales records and finds that jacket sales increase during colder months. Using this trend, the business can:

  1. Order more jackets before the colder season begins.
  2. Schedule advertisements when demand is expected to rise.
  3. Send jacket offers to customers who previously purchased similar clothing.
  4. Reduce unnecessary advertising to customers less likely to be interested.
⭐ Important concept

Buying trend analysis turns raw sales data into useful information for strategic decisions. It helps managers understand what customers buy, when they buy and who is most likely to buy.

Section 05

Improving Customer Service

5.1 What is customer service?

Customer service is the assistance and support a business provides to customers before, during and after they purchase its products or services.

Good customer service helps customers feel valued and encourages them to develop positive relationships with a business.

5.2 How do CRM systems improve customer service?

CRM systems store customer information and previous interactions. Employees can view relevant purchase histories, earlier enquiries and complaints to understand a customer's situation and offer more appropriate assistance.

5.3 Personalised customer experiences

🎂

Birthday greetings

Send a personalised birthday message or special offer to a customer.

✉️

Follow-up emails

Contact customers after a purchase to offer support or check satisfaction.

💬

Customer enquiries

Record questions so employees can respond and follow up effectively.

📋

Complaint tracking

Monitor reported problems and check whether they have been resolved.

5.4 Tracking customer issues

One important CRM function is recording and tracking customer problems. This allows issues to be followed up until they are resolved.

💡 Example — A faulty laptop complaint
  1. A customer contacts an electronics store about a faulty laptop.
  2. An employee records the complaint in the CRM system.
  3. The record includes purchase details and a description of the problem.
  4. The complaint is assigned to the appropriate employee or department.
  5. The CRM system records follow-up actions and the progress of the case.
  6. The business contacts the customer when the issue is resolved.

The CRM record helps prevent complaints from being forgotten and makes it easier to provide a satisfactory resolution.

5.5 Benefits of CRM for customer service

  • Faster and more informed responses to customer enquiries.
  • Accurate records of previous customer interactions.
  • Personalised support based on customer history and preferences.
  • Better follow-up of unresolved complaints and issues.
  • Improved customer satisfaction and overall service quality.

Section 06

Customer Retention

6.1 What is customer retention?

📌 Key definition — Customer retention

A business's ability to keep existing customers and encourage them to continue buying its products or services.

A retained customer returns to make further purchases instead of buying from a business only once.

6.2 Why is customer retention important?

Attracting a customer for one purchase may show that an advertisement or promotion worked. A returning customer, however, suggests satisfaction with a wider range of factors such as product quality, pricing, customer service and the overall shopping experience.

Returning customers may recommend the company to others, helping the business attract more customers through positive recommendations.

6.3 New customers versus returning customers

New customer Returning customer
Purchases from the business for the first time. Has purchased from the business before.
May be attracted by advertising or promotions. May return because of a positive previous experience.
Has limited purchase history with the business. Has an existing purchase history.
May be unfamiliar with the company's services. May already trust the company.

Both customer groups are valuable. However, retention helps a business build longer-term relationships and encourage repeat sales.

6.4 How do CRM systems improve customer retention?

CRM systems bring together information from across the business so that companies can understand and support existing customers. They can:

  • Identify customers who have not purchased anything recently.
  • Send personalised offers to encourage customers to return.
  • Reward regular customers through loyalty schemes.
  • Resolve customer complaints and service problems.
  • Recommend products based on previous purchases.
  • Maintain regular and relevant customer communication.

6.5 Example — Online bookstore

💡 Example — Encouraging a customer to return

A customer used to buy books regularly from an online bookstore but has not made a purchase for several months. The CRM system identifies the change in buying behaviour. The bookstore sends a discount offer for books in the customer's favourite genre. If the customer purchases again, the CRM-supported promotion has helped improve customer retention.

⭐ Important distinction

Customer acquisition means attracting new customers. Customer retention means encouraging existing customers to keep purchasing. CRM systems can support both, but retention focuses on long-term relationships.

Section 07

Upselling

7.1 What is upselling?

📌 Key definition — Upselling

A sales technique that encourages customers to spend more than they originally intended, such as by purchasing a more expensive product or additional items.

In the Topic 14.3 source, upselling is explained using recommendations for related add-on products that complement the original purchase.

CRM systems analyse customer purchase data and identify items that may be recommended to increase sales.

7.2 How do CRM systems support upselling?

CRM systems can be integrated with e-commerce websites. When a customer selects a product, the system can compare it with purchases made by other customers and identify related products that the customer may also want.

7.3 Example — Purchasing a laptop online

Example · CRM product recommendations
💻1. Customer chooses laptopAdds laptop to shopping basket
→
🧠2. CRM analyses dataChecks patterns in similar purchases
→
🛍️3. Recommends add-onsSuggests useful complementary products
💼Laptop caseProtection and transport
🖱️MouseExtra peripheral
🎧HeadsetAudio accessory

The customer may add recommended items, increasing the overall order value.

7.4 Why is upselling useful to businesses?

💰

Increases sales revenue

Customers may buy additional products or more expensive alternatives.

🎯

More relevant recommendations

Customers may discover useful products they had not considered.

📊

Uses existing data

Recommendations are based on purchasing patterns rather than guesswork.

⚡

Encourages impulse purchases

Customers may decide to add products they had not planned to buy.

Additional sales may help the company increase its profitability.

7.5 Upselling and cross-selling

Technique Meaning Example
Upselling Encouraging customers to purchase a more expensive or upgraded product. Recommending a laptop with more RAM and storage.
Cross-selling Encouraging customers to purchase additional related products. Recommending a laptop bag, mouse or headset.
⭐ Terminology note

The Topic 14.3 source calls its laptop-accessories example upselling. In standard sales terminology, recommending complementary accessories is more precisely called cross-selling. Both techniques aim to increase the value of a customer's purchase.

Section 08

How the Six Functions of CRM Work Together

The six CRM functions are interconnected. Information collected to support one activity may also be useful for other parts of the business.

Consider a supermarket that uses a CRM system across marketing, sales and customer support.

CRM function How the supermarket uses it
1. Synchronising marketing events Coordinates promotional messages across email, SMS and social media.
2. Loyalty schemes Awards points and offers personalised vouchers.
3. Buying trends Identifies popular products and helps plan future stock requirements.
4. Customer service Records customer questions, complaints and follow-up actions.
5. Customer retention Encourages previous shoppers to return through rewards and offers.
6. Upselling Recommends additional products using buying patterns.
📌 Key idea — Integrated systems

A supermarket does not need six separate CRM systems. One integrated CRM system may support multiple functions using information from shared databases and connected systems such as EPOS and e-commerce platforms.

Overall Understanding of Topic 14.3

Customer Relationship Management systems help organisations use customer data effectively.

They do more than store names and contact details. They analyse purchasing behaviour, coordinate marketing activities, personalise services and support long-term customer relationships.
