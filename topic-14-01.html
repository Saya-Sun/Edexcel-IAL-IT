The Role of IT Systems in Organisations

INTRODUCTION

💻IT Systems in Organisations

Almost every modern business and organisation relies on Information Technology (IT) systems to carry out its activities.

IT systems help organisations manage information, automate tasks, communicate with employees, make decisions, develop products and deliver services to customers.

Key Definition — IT System

An IT system is a combination of hardware, software, data, people and procedures that work together to collect, process, store and communicate information.

🌱 Example: A supermarket

A supermarket uses IT systems to record sales, manage stock, process payments and analyse customer purchasing behaviour. These systems help employees do their work and help managers make informed decisions.

IT systems are important because they enable organisations to improve efficiency, reduce costs, increase productivity, provide reliable services and remain competitive.

ROLE 01 · EVERYDAY BUSINESS ACTIVITIES

⚙️1. Operational Support

What is Operational Support?

Key Definition — Operational Support

The use of IT systems to help an organisation carry out its daily activities effectively and efficiently.

Every organisation performs routine activities such as processing payments, recording customer orders, managing employees, monitoring stock and preparing reports. Without suitable IT systems, many activities would take longer and be more prone to human error.

Main goals of operational support

Goal Explanation
Improve efficiency and productivity Complete more work in less time using fewer resources.
Improve decision-making Provide accurate and up-to-date information for managers.
Enhance communication and collaboration Help employees exchange information and work together.
Reduce costs and increase profits Reduce unnecessary labour, waste and operating expenses.
Improve customer service Offer faster and more reliable support to customers.
Ensure security and compliance Protect information and support compliance with relevant rules and regulations.

1.1 Automation

Key Definition — Automation

The use of technology to complete tasks with little or no human intervention.

Organisations repeatedly perform activities that may be slow, expensive or tiring when completed manually. Automating these tasks frees employees to focus on work requiring judgement, creativity and personal interaction.

Task How IT systems automate it
Data entry Automatically transfer information between systems.
Report generation Produce sales, attendance or financial reports automatically.
Order processing Record and process orders with little manual intervention.
Customer service Chatbots answer common customer questions.
Stock management Update stock quantities following sales.
Payment processing Record successful transactions automatically.

🌱 Example: Automation in a supermarket

When a customer buys an item, an Electronic Point of Sale (EPOS) system can perform the following sequence:

Scan barcode
→
Retrieve price
→
Calculate total
→
Record sale & update stock
→
Generate receipt

This reduces manual work, improves accuracy and helps the supermarket serve customers quickly.

✓ Benefits of automation

  • Higher productivity
  • Reduced human error
  • Lower operating costs over time
  • Consistent processing
  • More time for skilled work

⚠ Limitations

  • Expensive implementation
  • Failures may interrupt operations
  • Some situations still require staff
  • Employees may need training

1.2 Monitoring and Maintenance

Monitoring

Continuously checking the performance and condition of IT systems or equipment to detect unusual behaviour and potential problems.

Maintenance

Taking action to keep systems working correctly or to repair faults when they occur.

Monitoring software may collect CPU and memory usage, network traffic, server availability, storage capacity, machine temperature and error reports.

🌱 Example: Monitoring a company server

An online shopping company monitors its website server 24 hours a day. When CPU usage becomes unusually high, the monitoring software alerts a technician. The technician investigates before the server becomes overloaded and the website stops responding.

This is an example of preventive maintenance: action is taken before a major failure occurs.

Benefits

  • Reduces unexpected downtime and loss of service.
  • Helps prevent data loss and disruption.
  • Identifies potential problems early.
  • Increases reliability and can reduce repair costs.
Remember: Monitoring detects performance changes or problems. Maintenance involves preventing or resolving those problems.

1.3 Customer Service

Customer service is the assistance provided to customers before, during and after they purchase a product or use a service. IT systems make customer support more accessible, quicker and more convenient.

🌐 Self-service portals

Let customers manage services independently, e.g. track orders, change bookings or request refunds.

🤖 Chatbots

Automatically answer routine questions, e.g. delivery charges and business hours.

📚 Knowledge bases

Provide help articles, FAQs and troubleshooting instructions for customers.

Benefits

  • Customers may get support at any time.
  • Routine enquiries can be resolved immediately.
  • Staff can focus on complex requests.
  • Organisations can support more customers and improve satisfaction.
Limitation: Automated support does not always understand complicated problems. Organisations should provide a way to contact a human employee when needed.

1.4 Data Management and Analysis

Data Management

Collecting, storing, organising, updating and protecting organisational data.

Data Analysis

Examining data to identify patterns, trends and relationships that can support decisions.

Organisations collect customer information, sales transactions, employee attendance records, stock levels, visitor statistics and customer feedback.

🌱 Example: Clothing shop sales

A clothing shop analyses monthly sales and finds that jacket sales increase during colder months. The manager orders additional jackets before the next cold season. Data analysis helps the shop anticipate customer demand instead of relying only on guesses.

How analysis supports organisations

  • Identifies problems or areas needing improvement.
  • Reveals buying patterns and predicts demand.
  • Supports more efficient resource allocation.
  • Helps reduce costs and improve services or products.
Important: Decisions based on data are only reliable when the data is accurate, complete, relevant and sufficiently up to date.

1.5 Resource Management

Key Definition — Resource Management

The process of planning, allocating, using and monitoring the resources needed by an organisation.

👥 Human resources

Employees, skills and working time.

💰 Financial resources

Money, budgets and funding.

🏭 Physical resources

Buildings, machines and equipment.

📦 Material resources

Stock, components and raw materials.

Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) Systems

Key Definition — ERP

An Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system is an integrated software system that manages multiple business functions through a centralised system and shared information.

🗄️ Centralised ERP System
💰 Finance👥 Human Resources🚚 Supply Chain

ERP helps business departments share consistent, up-to-date information.

🌱 Example: ERP in a furniture company

  1. Sales records an order for 50 tables.
  2. The inventory system checks available wood.
  3. Purchasing orders more material if necessary.
  4. Production schedules manufacturing.
  5. Finance records costs and prepares the invoice.

Departments can coordinate because they use shared information.

Advantages of ERP

  • Reduces duplicated data and inconsistency.
  • Improves communication across departments.
  • Gives managers a wider view of business operations.
  • Supports planning, decisions and allocation of resources.
Potential challenge: ERP systems can be complex and expensive to introduce, and staff usually need training.
ROLE 02 · WORKING TOGETHER

🤝2. Collaboration

What is Collaboration?

Key Definition — Collaboration

The process of two or more people working together to achieve a common objective.

Employees must communicate, share files, exchange ideas and coordinate activities. IT systems support collaboration even when team members are located in different offices, cities or countries. This makes distributed teams and remote working more practical.

2.1 Communication Tools

Communication tools allow people to exchange messages, information and ideas within and between organisations.

Tool How it works Example
Email Sends electronic messages and attachments. A manager shares a monthly report.
Instant messaging Exchanges text messages in near real time. Team members discuss an urgent issue.
Video conferencing Connects people using live audio and video. Staff attend a remote meeting.

🌱 Example: International meetings

A business with offices in Myanmar and Singapore uses video conferencing to hold meetings. Workers share screens, discuss projects and agree on decisions without travelling between countries.

Benefits

  • Faster communication and coordination.
  • Supports remote and international teams.
  • Reduces travel time and expense.
  • Makes information sharing more flexible.

2.2 Collaboration Platforms

Key Definition — Collaboration Platform

An online environment that allows users to store, share, edit and manage files while working together.

Examples include Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 (previously Office 365) and Dropbox. Available features differ between products and plans.

📤 File sharing

Upload documents and provide access to authorised colleagues.

✍️ Real-time editing

Several employees can update the same document at once.

☁️ Cloud storage

Files can be accessed from different places and devices, subject to access and connectivity.

🕘 Version control

Track changes and restore earlier versions where supported.

🔒 Access permissions

Control who can view, comment on, edit or manage shared files.

💬 Comments

Discuss suggestions and feedback within shared documents.

🌱 Example: Writing a project report

Three employees edit different sections of the same shared report. Everyone sees updated content without repeatedly emailing copies. If someone accidentally deletes important material, version history may help restore it.

Benefits

  • Reduces confusion over document versions.
  • Supports simultaneous teamwork from different locations.
  • Reduces the need for email attachments.
  • Improves productivity and coordination.

2.3 Social Media and Enterprise Social Networks

Key Definition — Enterprise Social Network

A platform used within an organisation to encourage communication, idea sharing, informal networking and cooperation among employees.

Features may include group conversations, discussions, news feeds, announcements, communities and file sharing.

🌱 Example: Employee communities

Employees use Microsoft Teams channels to exchange ideas and seek help from colleagues across departments. A new employee can ask a question in a relevant group instead of contacting each department separately.

Benefits

  • Promotes the sharing of knowledge and ideas.
  • Improves communication across departments and hierarchy.
  • Helps remote employees build relationships.
  • Can stimulate innovation and teamwork.

2.4 Project Management Software

Key Definition — Project Management Software

Software that helps teams plan, organise, assign, schedule and track the work needed to complete a project.

Feature Purpose
Task assignment Allocates activities to specific team members.
Deadline management Sets and tracks task completion dates.
Progress tracking Shows finished, ongoing and overdue tasks.
Scheduling Organises when activities should start and finish.
Resource allocation Assigns workers, time and other resources.
Team discussions Allows communication about particular tasks or projects.

🌱 Example: Website development

A project manager assigns page design to a designer, programming to developers, content to writers and testing to a quality assurance team. Each task has a deadline and a progress status, helping the manager identify delays.

Project management software helps clarify responsibilities, coordinate work, identify late tasks and support timely completion.

Case Study: Fatimah's IT Training Company

Fatimah operates an IT training company with ten tutors. The company provides examination tutoring and business IT training. She plans to close its city-centre office and operate entirely remotely, while tutors continue to create and share learning resources.

Business activity Suitable IT solution
Teaching students remotely Video conferencing for live lessons.
Sharing resources Cloud storage for notes, slides and exercises.
Planning lessons together Shared editable documents.
Organising appointments Shared calendars and scheduling systems.
Assigning tutor responsibilities Project management software.
Daily communication Email and instant messaging.
Protecting student information Secure storage, authentication and access permissions.

✅ Why this plan helps

Collaboration software would allow Fatimah and her tutors to work from different locations while coordinating classes, preparing lessons and supporting clients. It could reduce office costs without abandoning key business tasks.

ROLE 03 · USING ORGANISATIONAL KNOWLEDGE

🧠3. Knowledge Management

What is Knowledge Management?

Key Definition — Knowledge Management

The process of organising, processing, storing, sharing and using information and knowledge effectively within an organisation.

Collecting large amounts of data is not enough. Employees need to find, interpret and apply useful information. Knowledge management helps an organisation retain expertise and use it to support decisions and tasks.

3.1 Data, Information and Knowledge

DATA45, 65, 70, 80, 90
→
INFORMATIONAverage examination mark: 70
→
KNOWLEDGEA student scoring 45 may need support

From raw facts, to processed information, to understanding that supports action.

Data

Raw facts, figures and observations without sufficient context to explain their meaning.

Information

Data that has been processed or given context so it becomes meaningful.

Knowledge

Understanding gained by interpreting information and applying experience or reasoning.

🌱 Example: Student test results

Data: 45, 65, 70, 80, 90.
Information: These are five students' marks and the average is 70.
Knowledge: A teacher recognises that the lowest-scoring student may need extra learning support and plans a suitable response.

Remember: Data = raw facts; information = meaningful processed data; knowledge = understanding that helps people make decisions or take action.

3.2 Document Management Systems (DMS)

Key Definition — DMS

A Document Management System (DMS) stores, organises, manages and retrieves electronic documents efficiently.

Organisations use a DMS for documents such as contracts, invoices, employee records, reports, policies and project files.

DMS feature What it does
Centralised storage Keeps documents in an organised digital location.
Version control Tracks document changes and may restore previous versions.
Document tagging Adds labels or keywords to simplify organisation.
Full-text search Finds specific words or phrases inside documents.
Access control Limits viewing, editing or deletion to authorised users.

🌱 Example: Hospital administration

A hospital uses a DMS to store administrative documents and staff policies. Authorised employees can find files using electronic search rather than looking through paper folders. Access permissions help protect confidential material.

Benefits

  • Documents can be found more quickly.
  • Reduces paper storage needs and duplicate copies.
  • Improves document security and organisation.
  • Supports collaboration and change tracking.

3.3 Content Management Systems (CMS)

Key Definition — CMS

A Content Management System (CMS) allows users to create, edit, organise, manage and publish digital content, often for websites.

CMS content can include web pages, articles, blogs, images, videos, online guides and wiki material.

Typical CMS features

  • Create and edit content through an interface.
  • Publish, update or remove articles and web pages.
  • Manage images and other media.
  • Support multiple authors and editors.
  • Keep revision history and control user permissions.

🌱 Example: A school's website

Teachers use a CMS to publish school announcements, upcoming events, photographs and information pages without manually editing HTML for every update.

A CMS makes publication easier, allows faster updates and helps maintain consistent online information.

3.4 Knowledge Repositories

Key Definition — Knowledge Repository

A centralised collection of documents, information and organisational knowledge that people can search, access and reuse.

Repositories often contain training manuals, research reports, troubleshooting guides, company policies, best-practice advice and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

A written set of instructions describing the steps that should be followed when performing a particular routine task.

🌱 Example: IT support company

Support staff search a repository for troubleshooting instructions when customers report common computer problems. They can solve problems using accurate, standard procedures instead of starting from scratch.

Benefits

  • Preserves expertise, even when an employee leaves.
  • Supports the training of new workers.
  • Encourages consistent procedures and answers.
  • Reduces repeated mistakes and duplicate effort.
  • Makes useful organisational knowledge easier to access.

Comparing DMS, CMS and Knowledge Repositories

System Main purpose Example use
DMS Manage, protect and retrieve electronic documents. Contracts, invoices and reports.
CMS Create, edit and publish digital content. Website pages and blog articles.
Knowledge repository Preserve and share useful expertise and reference material. Staff manuals and troubleshooting guides.
Key difference: A DMS focuses mainly on documents, a CMS on digital content and publication, and a repository on knowledge sharing and reuse. These systems may overlap: for example, a repository may use DMS or CMS technology.
ROLE 04 · CREATING AND IMPROVING PRODUCTS

🛠️4. Product Development

What is Product Development?

Key Definition — Product Development

The process of creating a new product or improving an existing product to meet customer needs and organisational objectives.

Businesses develop products to respond to changing needs, improve quality, introduce features, increase sales and remain competitive. IT systems support research, planning, design, testing and production.

The four main stages

1Ideation, Conceptualisation and PlanningExplore and assess an idea
2Design and PrototypingSpecify and trial early designs
3Development and TestingBuild, test and improve
4Production and ManufacturingProduce physical goods consistently

Stage 1 — Ideation, Conceptualisation and Planning

Ideation

Generating possible product or feature ideas.

Conceptualisation

Developing an early idea into a clearer product concept.

Planning

Identifying requirements, resources, schedules and proposed tasks.

Main activities

  1. Identify customer problems and needs.
  2. Brainstorm possible ideas.
  3. Research the market and competitors.
  4. Consult stakeholders.
  5. Carry out feasibility studies.
  6. Estimate costs, resources and timelines.
  7. Refine and select promising ideas.
Feasibility Study

An investigation into whether a proposed product is practical and achievable using the available time, money, technology and other resources.

Stakeholder

A person or group with an interest in, or who may be affected by, the product, such as customers, managers, employees or investors.

How IT systems help

Online surveys, research databases, spreadsheets, communication tools and project planning systems help teams collect opinions, compare options, forecast costs and organise early work.

🌱 Example: Planning a mobile learning app

An education company surveys learners, researches competing applications, holds online meetings with teachers and developers, and uses spreadsheets to estimate development costs. The company can then judge whether the idea is feasible.

Stage 2 — Design and Prototyping

Design

Preparing detailed specifications for how a product looks, functions and operates.

Prototyping

Creating an early or simplified version of a product to test ideas and gather feedback.

Main activities

  • Prepare sketches, diagrams and designs.
  • Specify features and system requirements.
  • Build early prototypes.
  • Collect feedback from users and stakeholders.
  • Improve designs, sometimes through several iterations.
  • Approve an appropriate design for development.

How IT systems help

Computer-Aided Design (CAD), diagramming and flowchart tools, user-interface design tools, prototyping applications and shared design platforms can help develop and refine designs.

🌱 Example: Student registration application

Developers create prototype registration, login and dashboard screens. Teachers and students review these screens and suggest changes. The team improves its design before completing the application.

Functional and Non-Functional Requirements

Functional Requirements

Define what a system must do.

  • Register a new student account.
  • Allow users to log in.
  • Let teachers approve registrations.
  • Display learning materials.
  • Let students update profiles.

Non-Functional Requirements

Define how well a system must operate or its overall qualities.

  • Load pages within a defined time.
  • Protect private user data.
  • Support a stated number of users.
  • Work well on mobile devices.
  • Meet an availability target.
Functional requirements Non-functional requirements
Describe system functions and features. Describe quality and operating characteristics.
Example: Allow users to log in. Example: Process normal login requests within two seconds.
Example: Generate a report. Example: Restrict report access to authorised users.
Why both matter: A system could include all the required features but still be unsuitable because it is too slow, insecure or difficult to use.

Stage 3 — Development and Testing

Development

Building and implementing the product according to the approved design and requirements.

Testing

Checking that the product works correctly, meets requirements and contains as few significant defects as practical.

Main activities

  1. Build or implement the product.
  2. Develop required features.
  3. Test individual components.
  4. Test components working together.
  5. Identify bugs and other problems.
  6. Correct errors and retest.
  7. Gather feedback from potential users.

How IT systems help

Programming environments, automated test software, simulators, issue-tracking software and collaboration tools help teams develop, test and improve their work.

🌱 Example: Online shopping application

During testing, developers discover that a shopping application calculates some delivery charges incorrectly. The problem is repaired before release, avoiding incorrect charges and potential customer complaints.

Why testing during development matters

  • Identifies defects before the product is released.
  • Problems are often cheaper to fix earlier.
  • User feedback can improve usability and quality.
  • Shows whether specifications and expectations are being met.
  • Reduces the risk of major failures after release.
Important: Testing is not just a final activity. It should take place throughout development, and changes should be retested.

Stage 4 — Production and Manufacturing

Key Definition — Production and Manufacturing

The process of producing finished products efficiently and consistently. For physical goods, this may include machining, assembly and quality inspection.

This stage is especially relevant to physical products such as smartphones, cars and electronic devices. IT systems can keep production processes consistent and help monitor product quality.

🏭 Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM)

Computer systems help control or assist manufacturing machinery.

🤖 Robotic systems

Programmable machines handle tasks such as assembly, welding or packaging.

📊 Production monitoring

Systems track machinery and processes to identify changes or problems.

✅ Quality control

Systems support checks that products meet specified standards.

🌱 Example: Smartphone manufacturing

A manufacturer uses automated equipment to assemble electronic components. Computer systems control operations, monitor the production line and support quality checks, helping manufacture smartphones consistently.

Benefits

  • Faster production.
  • More consistent product characteristics.
  • Fewer manufacturing errors and less waste.
  • Better equipment monitoring and reliability.
  • Improved quality control.
Digital products: A software application does not require a physical manufacturing line. Its final stage may involve packaging, deployment and distribution instead.

Products and Services

Product Service
Something created and supplied to customers. An activity or benefit provided to customers.
Can be physical or digital. Generally intangible.
Examples: Laptop, smartphone, mobile app. Examples: Tutoring, support, online consultation.
A customer obtains or uses the product. A customer receives the benefit of the activity.

🌱 Example: An education business

A learning application is a digital product. Live online tuition is a service. A company may offer both products and services.

ROLE 05 · PROVIDING SERVICES

🚀5. Service Delivery

What is Service Delivery?

Key Definition — Service Delivery

The process through which an organisation provides its services to customers or users.

IT systems provide platforms and tools for delivering services efficiently and to people in more locations. They support automation, effective data and knowledge management and better organisation of workflows.

About this section: The supplied Topic 14.1 textbook extract introduces service delivery briefly. The explanations and examples below expand on that introduction to make it easier to understand.

5.1 How IT Systems Improve Service Delivery

⚙️ A. Process Automation

Routine service steps can happen automatically. An online booking platform may confirm an appointment, update a database and send a notification.

🌍 B. Wider Customer Reach

Online systems help organisations serve customers in different cities or countries without requiring physical travel.

🗃️ C. Data Management

Customer and service records can be stored, retrieved and updated quickly, helping staff provide accurate support.

📚 D. Knowledge Management

Guides, manuals and knowledge repositories help employees answer enquiries consistently and quickly.

🔁 E. Workflow Optimisation

Digital systems can reduce unnecessary steps, delays and duplication in the sequence of tasks needed to provide a service.

Workflow Optimisation

Improving the sequence of activities in a process to reduce unnecessary work, delays and inefficiencies.

5.2 Examples of Service Delivery

Organisation Service Role of IT systems
Bank Online banking Customers check balances and transfer money electronically.
School Online learning Students access lessons, submit assignments and receive feedback.
Hospital Appointments Patients request or manage bookings online.
Retail business Online shopping Customers place orders and track deliveries.
Transport company Ticket booking Customers search journeys and reserve tickets.

🌱 Example: An online learning platform

An education company may deliver its teaching service through a series of connected digital processes:

Student registers
→
System stores details
→
Student studies materials
→
Attends online lesson
→
Completes assessment
→
Teacher reviews progress

The platform helps connect registration, learning activities, assessment and feedback into a manageable service.

5.3 Benefits and Challenges

Benefits Challenges
Services may be available 24 hours a day. Outages may interrupt access.
Customers can access services remotely. Customers may need internet access and suitable devices.
Routine activities may be completed faster. Implementation and ongoing support cost money.
Organisations may reduce operating costs. Cybersecurity and privacy risks need controls.
Services can reach a larger audience. Some customers may lack the necessary digital skills.
Data can improve service quality. Incorrect or outdated data can reduce service quality.
PUTTING THE IDEAS TOGETHER

🔗6. How the Five Roles Work Together

Although the roles are studied separately, organisations commonly use connected IT systems to support several roles at the same time.

🌱 Example: Online education company

Consider a company that produces online learning resources, hires tutors and teaches students through an internet platform.

Role Example within the education company
Operational support Automates student registration, records payments and maintains class timetables.
Collaboration Teachers share resources, hold online meetings and plan lessons together.
Knowledge management Stores teaching guides, policies, lesson documents and training resources.
Product development Plans, designs, builds, tests and improves the company's learning application.
Service delivery Gives students access to online classes, learning materials and teaching support.

When the company introduces a new online course, teachers may collaborate on resources, consult stored teaching knowledge, develop and test materials, and deliver the course through the learning platform. Operational systems support enrolment, scheduling and payment throughout.

Core understanding: IT systems do not simply carry out isolated tasks. They can connect an organisation's operations, employees, information, products and services to support its overall success.
